Web development for freight and B2B SaaS

Quote, track, and sign up without a phone call.

Next.js sites and public web surfaces for trucking, logistics, auto-transport, and B2B SaaS teams: quote and booking requests, shipment tracking pages, carrier sign-up front doors, and the marketing site around them. Screens behind a login are designed under web application design.

Who this web development service is for, what ships, and what it does not cover

Who it's for

Freight brokers, carriers, auto-transport companies, and 3PLs whose website is the first place a shipper, driver, or owner-operator looks. Also B2B SaaS teams that need a public site and product pages their own marketers can edit.

What we deliver

Public web surfaces in Next.js, React, and TypeScript: quote and booking request flows, shipment lookup pages, carrier sign-up intake, service and lane pages, and a CMS when marketing must publish without engineering.

What it does not cover

Dispatcher consoles, carrier or customer portals behind a login, and driver apps are separate work. We point you to web application design or mobile apps in the first call if that is the real gap.

KRIVA is a product design and engineering studio, not a theme shop. You own the code, components, and CMS configuration after launch.

Where public pages lose freight

Four pages that quietly
create phone calls.

01

The quote request that arrives as a long email

A generic contact form does not ask for lanes, equipment type, pickup window, or commodity. Dispatch calls back to collect what the form should have captured.Fix: a structured quote or booking flow

02

"Where is my load?" calls

Customers have no self-serve way to check status, so customer service answers the same question all day. A tracking page with clear status and exception wording takes that load off the phones.Needs: a status source your system can expose

03

Carrier onboarding that starts with a PDF

New carriers download a packet, email it back, and wait. A sign-up front door collects company details and documents in one pass and hands a clean record to whoever verifies it.Verification stays with your team

04

A site built for 1440px, used from a phone

Drivers, owner-operators, and shippers on the dock open your site on a phone. Tap targets, tables, and forms need their own layout, not a shrunken desktop page.Designed as a real surface

What we build

The public layer in front
of your operations.

Fast, indexable pages that talk to the systems you already run, with SaaS marketing sites and product pages built the same way.

Stack

Next.js, React, TypeScript

  • Server-rendered pages with a component library your product team can extend
  • Structured data and SEO foundations set up during the build
  • Analytics events on quote, booking, and sign-up steps
Connections

Forms and status that go somewhere

  • Quote and booking forms post to the endpoint, email, or CRM you choose
  • Tracking pages read from an API or feed your system provides
  • Anything deeper is scoped with integrations and APIs
Publishing

CMS when marketing must move

  • Headless CMS for service pages, lane pages, and campaign landing pages
  • Preview and publish flows matched to how your team works
  • SEO & digital marketing is separate when you want ongoing search work

How we work

Inventory, build, hand over.

Short loop with demos along the way. The shape of the data your systems can expose decides most of the plan.

  1. 01 · Inventory

    Pages, forms, and data

    List every public page and form, who receives each submission, and which systems can feed status or rates. Gaps surface here, not in week six.

  2. 02 · Build

    Responsive Next.js

    Components, flows, and integrations built desktop and mobile together, with working previews you can click through each week.

  3. 03 · Hand over

    Publish and measure

    CMS training, analytics events, and a performance and SEO check before launch so you know what "good" looks like afterward.
