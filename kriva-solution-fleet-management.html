The problem

Why fleets outgrow their tools

Most fleets already own good point tools. The trouble starts in the gaps between them.

Vehicle data lives in five portals

ELD, GPS, fuel cards, the shop system, and accounting each have their own login, and their numbers rarely agree.

Maintenance runs on memory

Service intervals live on a whiteboard, so trucks come off the road when they break, not when you plan it.

Defects never reach the shop

A driver notes a problem on an inspection, and it stays on paper or in an app the mechanic never opens.

Compliance dates sneak up

Medical cards, permits, registrations, and annual inspections are tracked in a spreadsheet one person maintains.

IFTA is rebuilt every quarter

Miles and fuel by state are pieced together from exports just before filing. ELD logs are built for hours of service, not fuel tax, so they may not hold everything a filing needs.

Nobody trusts cost per mile

Without one source of data, the number changes depending on who pulled it and from which system.

Fleet visibility

Every system has the number. Someone still types it twice.

Fleet data rarely goes missing. It sits in the ELD portal, the fuel card statement, the shop's work orders, and the accounting file, and someone copies it between them every week. A custom fleet system reads those sources through their APIs or exports, so the copying stops and the figures match.

  • Vehicle, driver, fuel, and maintenance data joined by unit number
  • Imports from provider APIs or scheduled exports, checked for gaps
  • One cost-per-mile figure you can trace back to its sources

What we can scope with you

What a fleet management system can include

Pick the modules your operation needs. Most fleets start with two or three and add the rest in later phases.

Fleet operations dashboard

Every unit and driver on one screen, sorted by what needs action today: overdue service, open defects, and expiring documents.

Maintenance scheduling

Preventive maintenance by miles, engine hours, or date, with work orders, repair history, and reminders before a unit is due.

Inspections and defects

Digital inspection checklists, with defects routed to the shop and signed off when repaired. Your compliance owner decides how this fits your DVIR process.

Driver records and apps

Driver profiles, documents, and a mobile app for inspections and status updates that queues offline and syncs when the signal returns.

Compliance tracking

Expiry reminders for medical cards, licenses, permits, registrations, and annual inspections, plus records you can pull up for an audit.

Utilization and assignments

Which driver had which unit, miles and hours per vehicle, and which units sit idle, so decisions to add or retire trucks rest on data.

ELD, GPS, and fuel card data

We read data from your existing providers' APIs or exports. We don't sell ELD hardware, and you keep the providers you already use.

IFTA and fuel records

Miles and fuel by jurisdiction kept as trips happen, ready for your accountant or filing tool. We prepare the data; we don't file returns.

Cost and accounting sync

Fuel, maintenance, and repair costs connected to QuickBooks or Xero, so cost per mile comes from one source.

We scope each module with you during discovery, based on what your providers allow and what your operation needs. Compliance rules stay with your compliance owner, and we do not give legal or tax advice.

We build on the public Intuit and Xero APIs as an independent team, not as a partner of either company.

Maintenance coordination

From reported defect to repaired truck, in one record.

When a driver flags a defect, it should reach the shop as a work order, not a text message. Preventive maintenance should come due from mileage, engine hours, or date, before a breakdown takes the truck off the road.

  • Preventive maintenance due lists by miles, engine hours, or date
  • Inspection defects routed to the shop as work orders
  • Repair history, parts, and labor cost per unit

Driver management and compliance

Driver records ready before anyone asks for them.

Medical cards, licenses, permits, and annual inspections expire on their own schedules. One person's spreadsheet works until that person is on leave. Driver apps have the opposite problem: they are used one-handed, in a cab or a yard, often on a weak signal.

  • Expiry reminders for driver and vehicle documents, with an audit trail
  • A driver app for inspections, documents, and status that works offline
  • Your compliance owner sets the rules; the software keeps the records

The outcome

What this is designed to change

These are design goals, not reported results. We have not published fleet metrics, and we won't quote numbers we can't show you.

  • Fleet managers: One screen showing which units and drivers need attention today.
  • Maintenance leads: Planned service and a defect queue instead of roadside surprises.

An honest answer

Should a fleet build or buy?

Most fleets should start with an established telematics and ELD platform, and we won't try to replace that hardware. Custom software makes sense on top of it: when maintenance, compliance, and cost data cross several systems, when your mix of vehicles and equipment doesn't fit an off-the-shelf tool, or when the reports you need don't exist anywhere.

Will this replace our ELD or telematics provider?

No. We read data from your provider where its API or export allows it. ELD devices and their certification stay with your provider.

Which telematics, fuel card, and maintenance systems can you connect to?

Any provider that offers an API or a reliable data export. Access often depends on your plan with that provider, so we confirm what each one allows during discovery, before we quote.

Can driver inspections in the app replace our paper DVIRs?

We can build digital inspection checklists and route defects to the shop. Whether the app serves as your official DVIR record depends on your compliance requirements and ELD setup, so your compliance owner decides. Some fleets keep the DVIR in the ELD app and pull defects from there instead.

Can drivers use the app with poor signal?

That is a core design requirement for driver apps: updates queue offline and sync when the connection returns. We confirm the devices and conditions to test against during discovery.

Can one system cover trucks, trailers, vans, and equipment?

Usually, yes. The data model is built around the assets you actually run, so tractors, trailers, service vans, and yard equipment can share maintenance schedules and records while keeping the fields each type needs.

How long does a first version take?

A focused first version is usually 6 to 10 weeks after a 1 to 2 week discovery. A full platform with integrations usually runs 12 to 20 weeks.

How is pricing structured?

Fixed-scope projects, sprint-based work, or a monthly retainer. Everything is scoped after discovery, with deliverables and milestones agreed up front.

Who owns the software?

You do. Code, designs, and documentation transfer to you at project close or earlier.
