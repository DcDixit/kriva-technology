The problem

Why fleets outgrow their tools

Data is in five portals

ELD, fuel cards, maintenance, and accounting each have their own login, and none of them agree.

Maintenance runs on memory

Service schedules live on a whiteboard, so trucks come off the road when they break, not when you plan it.

Compliance dates sneak up

Permits, medical cards, and inspections are tracked in a spreadsheet one person maintains.

IFTA gets rebuilt every quarter

Miles and fuel by state are pieced together from exports just before filing. The federal ELD rule does not define tax mileage, so you can't assume your logs already cover it.

Drivers fight the app

Tools built for the office fail one-handed in a yard with a weak signal.

Nobody trusts cost per mile

Without one source of data, the number changes depending on who pulled it.

What we can scope with you

What a fleet system can include

Fleet operations dashboard

Vehicles, drivers, and exceptions on one screen, sorted by what needs action today rather than by chart.

Driver mobile apps

Status updates, documents, and proof of delivery that keep working offline and sync when the signal returns.

Maintenance records

Service schedules by mileage or date, work order history, and reminders before a truck is due.

Compliance tracking

Expiry reminders for permits, medical cards, and inspections, plus records you can pull up for an audit.

ELD, fuel, and telematics data

We read data from your existing provider's API. We don't sell ELD hardware, and you keep the provider you already use.

Cost and accounting sync

Fuel, maintenance, and settlement data connected to QuickBooks or Xero, so cost per mile has one source.

We scope each of these with you during discovery, based on what your providers allow and what your operation needs. Compliance rules stay with your compliance owner, and we do not give legal or tax advice.

We build on the public Intuit and Xero APIs as an independent team, not as a partner of either company.

The outcome

What this is designed to change

  • One place to see which trucks and drivers need attention today
  • Planned maintenance instead of roadside surprises
  • IFTA miles and fuel receipts kept by jurisdiction as trips happen, ready for your accountant or filing tool
  • Cost per mile that everyone pulls from the same data

An honest answer

Should a fleet build or buy?

Most fleets should start with an established telematics and ELD platform, and we won't try to replace that hardware. Custom software makes sense on top of it, when your workflows cross several systems, or the reports you need don't exist in any of them. We are not legal or tax advisers, so your compliance owner signs off on the rules.

Will this replace our ELD provider?

No. We read data from your provider where its API allows it. ELD devices and their certification stay with your provider.

Can drivers use the app with poor signal?

That is a core design requirement for driver apps: updates queue offline and sync when the connection returns. We confirm the devices and conditions to test against during discovery.

How long does a first version take?

A focused first version is usually 6 to 10 weeks after a 1 to 2 week discovery. A full platform with integrations usually runs 12 to 20 weeks.

Who owns the software?

You do. Code, designs, and documentation transfer to you at project close or earlier.
