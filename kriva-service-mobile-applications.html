Who this mobile app service is for, what we deliver, and where it stops

Who it's for

Carriers, brokers, and logistics operators whose drivers or field staff still call dispatch, text photos, or fill paper. Also B2B software companies that need a focused mobile companion to a desktop product.

What we deliver

App flows and screens built for weak signal and gloves-on use, POD and document capture, an offline queue, push where it earns its place, and the API contract that connects the phone to your TMS, CRM, or SaaS backend.

Where it stops

We do not build ELD hardware integrations or consumer-scale apps without a separate scope. Store review times and vendor APIs are outside our control, and we plan around them openly.

Designs, code, and documentation transfer to your accounts after launch.

Where driver apps fail

The app installs. The workflow still leaves the phone.

A small screen, a loud dock, a dead zone on the route. Apps that ignore those conditions lose the workflow back to phone calls and paper.

01

Desktop shrink

The dispatch table gets squeezed onto a phone. Buttons are small, text is dense, and nobody can use it with one hand on the wheel or a clipboard.COST: drivers go back to calls and paper

02

Delayed first action

Logins, tours, and menus stand between the driver and the one thing they opened the app to do, like accepting a load.COST: the first critical action never happens in the app

03

No offline recovery

A POD photo fails to upload from a yard with poor signal, and the app shows nothing about whether it will try again.COST: lost PODs, end-of-shift catch-up, invoices held

Who uses the app

Drivers, field staff, and account users need different apps.

A driver in a cab, a technician in sunlight, and a customer admin on a commute all open the phone for a different job.

Drivers: accept, update status, capture proof

  • Load acceptance with lane, stop, and appointment window
  • One-tap status progression from pickup to delivery
  • POD photos and signatures with an offline queue
  • Large touch targets for cab and dock use

Yard and field staff: job details and capture outdoors

  • Job location, access notes, and completion checklist
  • High-contrast UI readable in direct sunlight
  • Camera and document capture with save points
  • Interrupted workflows that resume where the user left off

B2B companions: approvals and lookups away from the desk

  • Approvals and quick record updates, not full admin
  • Push alerts tied to one clear next action
  • Account lookup without desktop navigation depth
  • Companion scope defined around mobile-sized jobs

Core mobile workflow

Accept, status, documents, recovery.

For each step we write down what the driver needs, what can fail, what happens offline, and what the backend must update. Dispatch and billing depend on these steps being right.

  1. 01 · Accept

    User confirms the job

    Driver goal: know the load, the stops, and the appointment window.

    • Essential details only
    • Location and time
    • Offline-safe confirmation
  2. 02 · Status

    User marks progress

    Driver goal: report arrival, loaded, and departed without typing.

    • Clear progression steps
    • Single tap per state
    • Offline queue on weak signal
  3. 03 · Documents

    User captures proof

    Driver goal: get the POD or BOL photo accepted the first time.

    • Camera or signature capture
    • Guidance on requirements
    • Offline storage and sync
  4. 04 · Recovery

    System handles failures

    Driver goal: see what uploaded, what is waiting, what needs a retry.

    • Clear state indicators
    • Retry without reentry
    • Audit trail visible

How we work

Name the job before choosing the platform.

Four stages. We define the user and the first critical action, prototype the interrupted and offline paths, build the app and the backend contract, then test and launch.

  1. 01

    Define and research

    User, first critical action, offline needs, camera and backend requirements. Platform choice is validated here.

  2. 02

    Prototype the hard paths

    Interrupted tasks, weak signal, camera, retry states. Tested with real drivers or field staff where we can.

  3. 03

    Build app and contract

    Screens, offline queue, API contract, error handling, and documentation, shown regularly on a test build.

  4. 04

    Launch and hand over

    Beta with a small group, store submission from your accounts, and a handover of code and designs.

You provide: API docs or a test environment 2-3 drivers or field users to test with Apple and Google developer accounts Client-owned code and design

Mobile app questions.

Platform choice, offline behavior, TMS fit, and store publishing.

It depends on the job. Heavy camera use, background location, Bluetooth hardware, or strict battery limits can push toward native. A cross-platform framework often fits when the screens are mostly forms, status, and document capture. We make the call after the first workshop and write down why.

The app saves the photo or signature on the phone, shows it as queued, and retries when signal returns. The driver sees what has uploaded and what is still waiting, and dispatch sees the same state. We agree conflict rules with you, such as what happens if the load was reassigned while the phone was offline.
