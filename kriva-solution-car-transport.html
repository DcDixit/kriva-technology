Core challenges

One order should not become five disconnected workflows.

When quoting, dispatch, and billing each keep their own copy of the order, the office spends its day reconciling instead of moving cars. These are the three failures we design against first.

01

The quote and the order disagree

The customer books at one price, then ops rebuilds the route, vehicle, and pickup window by hand and lands on another. That gap means repricing calls, cancelled bookings, and support time.

02

Carrier coordination happens in texts

Carrier assignment, driver updates, and changed pickup windows move into texts and spreadsheets. The system becomes a reference copy, and nobody trusts it as the source of truth.

03

Delivery does not close the order

The driver captures proof of delivery, but invoicing, the CRM, and customer tracking each need a separate manual update. One delivery event should close the order everywhere.

Quote to proof of delivery

One order. Four critical phases.

Every vehicle move passes through the same four handoffs. Each one has a different user, and each must finish cleanly before the next can start.

  1. 01Quote and book
  2. 02Assign and dispatch
  3. 03Pickup and transit
  4. 04Deliver and close
01 Quote and book

The quote form is the shop window.

Most customers request a quote from a phone. They enter vehicle, running condition, pickup and delivery locations, and a pickup window, then see an itemized price and can book and pay without calling.

Customer-facing
02 Assign and dispatch

Capacity, route, and equipment in one assignment view.

Dispatch matches the vehicle to a carrier who can actually move it: open or enclosed trailer, space on the truck, insurance on file, and a matching pickup window. Changes and exceptions are handled on the order, not in a text thread.

Ops console
03 Pickup and transit

Condition capture protects both sides.

The driver records photos, existing damage, and whether the vehicle runs, often one-handed in a driveway. If signal drops, updates queue on the phone and sync when it returns. At pickup, the condition report is the record both sides rely on.

Driver app
04 Deliver and close

POD closes the order and starts the invoice.

A signature and delivery photos close the move. That one event updates customer tracking, starts the invoice, and syncs to your CRM and accounting, so nobody re-keys the order.

At the handoff

Condition notes protect the carrier and the customer.

Vehicles arrive with protective wrap, existing marks, and a customer waiting at the curb. When the driver app records photos, notes, and a signature at both pickup and delivery, a damage question becomes a record both sides can check, not a disagreement.

  • Matching photo sets at pickup and delivery
  • Signature captured in the same flow
  • The record attached to the order, not a phone gallery

Integrations and reliability

The order stays connected after the booking.

We connect the order to your CRM, TMS, payments, and QuickBooks or Xero, with errors your team can see and retry instead of silent data drift.

Booking portal → Order record → Dispatch → Driver workflow → POD → Invoice · CRM · Accounting

Visible validation errors

If a quote cannot be honored or no carrier is available, the customer and ops see it before payment, not after.

Failed-sync states and retry

When a POD fails to create an invoice or a dispatch fails to reach the driver, the order shows it, with a retry your team can run.

One order ID across workflows

The same order ID in booking, dispatch, the driver app, tracking, and the invoice, so reconciliation takes a glance.

What we can connect depends on the API or data export each system offers. We confirm what your tools expose during discovery, before anything is scoped. See API integrations and QuickBooks and Xero integrations.
