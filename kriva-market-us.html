Operating conditions

What makes US trucking builds different.

US carriers and brokers run inside a federal compliance layer that most generic CRMs ignore. The work that matters is getting hours, fuel, and load data into the same record the dispatcher already uses. We build the screens and data model that record these obligations; we do not give legal or tax advice, and your compliance owner signs off the rules.

  • ELD and hours-of-service data next to the load, not in a separate portal
  • IFTA miles and fuel records by jurisdiction, where your build needs them
  • Load board, factoring, and broker-carrier workflows in one view
  • QuickBooks Online invoicing and settlement sync
  • Central, Mountain, and Pacific drivers on one Eastern-time desk

Hours-of-service aware dispatch

A driver with forty minutes of drive time left is not available for a three-hour lane, even if the board says they are free. We pull remaining hours from your ELD provider’s API where one exists and show it on the assignment row, so the dispatcher sees the constraint before booking the load rather than after the driver calls in.

IFTA and mileage records

Quarterly IFTA filing goes wrong when miles by state live in a spreadsheet someone rebuilds every quarter. A custom build can store jurisdiction miles and fuel purchases against the trip as they happen, with an export your accountant or filing tool can use, and a clear flag on trips with missing data.

Workflow automation →

Broker, carrier, and factoring flow

Rate confirmation, proof of delivery, and the invoice that goes to a factoring company are three documents describing one load. We link them to the same record, so billing does not wait on someone matching PDFs by hand, and the carrier pay status is visible to the desk.

QuickBooks Online sync that can be audited

Invoices and settlements sync to QuickBooks Online through its official API, with a log of every record that synced, failed, or needs review. Finance can retry a failed sync without engineering help and can trace any total back to the loads behind it.
