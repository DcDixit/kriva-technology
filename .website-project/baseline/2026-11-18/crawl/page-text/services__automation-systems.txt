Describe the workflow → 01 Will automation message our customers or brokers on its own? Not for anything that carries money, a commitment, or a complaint. Status copies, internal flags, and document filing can run without a person. A customer-facing message, a detention claim, or an invoice waits in an approval queue until someone on your team sends it. If you want one narrow message to go automatically, we scope it with explicit rules and an off switch. 02 Which steps should we automate first? Start with work that is repeated, rule-based, and cheap to get wrong: copying load status into a sheet, filing PODs against the right load, flagging a missed check call. Leave judgment calls, such as reassigning a late load or waiving detention, with your dispatchers and billing team. We confirm the list during the workflow map. 03 Make, Zapier, or custom code? Make or Zapier when the volume, connectors, and approval steps fit inside the platform and your team will maintain it. Custom workers when run limits, data handling, or the approval console outgrow the platform. We check run limits, connector coverage, and data terms before recommending one. 04 What if our TMS or load board has no API? It happens often. We read the vendor's API docs and your account permissions first. If there is no usable API, the fallbacks are scheduled exports, email parsing, or a small import step, each with more failure modes. If none of those is reliable enough, we will say the step should stay manual. 05 How is this different from Integrations & APIs and AI-Assisted Development? This service designs the queue: triggers, approvals, and what happens when a run fails. Integrations & APIs covers connector architecture between systems. AI-Assisted Development covers how AI is used to build and inside your product. We may use an LLM step inside a scenario, with a person approving the output. 06 Who owns the workflow after launch? Your team. The scenarios live in your Make, Zapier, or hosting accounts. We hand over the workflow map, naming, run-failure queue, and a short runbook.
