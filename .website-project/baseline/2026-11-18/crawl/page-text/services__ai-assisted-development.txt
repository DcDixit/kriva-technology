THE GATE If it can reach a customer, a carrier, or the ledger, a person reviews it. AI is good at drafting, sorting, and summarizing. It is not accountable for a wrong invoice, a missed appointment, or a bad answer to a customer. We design the review step so a person is. WHERE AI HELPS IN LOGISTICS SOFTWARE Four jobs worth scoping, each with a person at the end. These are patterns we design and scope with you, not packaged products. The right ones depend on your documents, systems, and risk. 01 Exception summaries for dispatch Turn a pile of status notes, emails, and timestamps into a short plain-language summary of why a load is at risk. The dispatcher reads it and decides whether to reassign or call the customer. 02 Document intake Read rate confirmations, BOLs, and PODs into fields on a review screen. Low-confidence fields are flagged for a person. Nothing is posted to the load or the ledger until someone confirms it. 03 Detention and accessorial claims Compare arrival and release times to the appointment window and draft the claim. Billing reviews, edits, and approves before it goes to the broker or shipper. 04 Invoice and settlement exceptions Group mismatched lines and likely causes for a finance person to resolve. Writes to QuickBooks or Xero happen after approval, not before. The same pattern works for SaaS: support triage drafts, onboarding hints, and account summaries. SupportAI is our published example. WHAT WE WILL NOT AUTOMATE UNSUPERVISED Speed is not a substitute for ownership. 01 · SEND Customer and carrier messages Drafts, yes. Sending needs a person. That includes detention claims and anything that states a commitment. 02 · BOOKS Ledger and settlement postings Exceptions go to an operator. A model does not close the books or release a driver settlement. 03 · PRODUCT “The AI built it” Tools speed up the work. Designers and engineers still own the file, the spec, and the merge.
