The trouble starts in the gaps between them. 01 Vehicle data lives in five portals ELD, GPS, fuel cards, the shop system, and accounting each have their own login, and their numbers rarely agree. 02 Maintenance runs on memory Service intervals live on a whiteboard, so trucks come off the road when they break, not when you plan it. 03 Defects never reach the shop A driver notes a problem on an inspection, and it stays on paper or in an app the mechanic never opens. 04 Compliance dates sneak up Medical cards, permits, registrations, and annual inspections are tracked in a spreadsheet one person maintains. 05 IFTA is rebuilt every quarter Miles and fuel by state are pieced together from exports just before filing. ELD logs are built for hours of service, not fuel tax, so they may not hold everything a filing needs. 06 Nobody trusts cost per mile Without one source of data, the number changes depending on who pulled it and from which system. FLEET VISIBILITY Every system has the number. Someone still types it twice. Fleet data rarely goes missing. It sits in the ELD portal, the fuel card statement, the shop's work orders, and the accounting file, and someone copies it between them every week. A custom fleet system reads those sources through their APIs or exports, so the copying stops and the figures match. AN HONEST ANSWER Should a fleet build or buy? Most fleets should start with an established telematics and ELD platform, and we won't try to replace that hardware. Custom software makes sense on top of it: when maintenance, compliance, and cost data cross several systems, when your mix of vehicles and equipment doesn't fit an off-the-shelf tool, or when the reports you need don't exist anywhere.
