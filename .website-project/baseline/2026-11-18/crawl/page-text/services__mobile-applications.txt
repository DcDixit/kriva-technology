Platform choice, offline behavior, TMS fit, and store publishing. Photograph via Unsplash License. Not a KRIVA client or product. 01 Native, React Native, or Flutter for a driver app? It depends on the job. Heavy camera use, background location, Bluetooth hardware, or strict battery limits can push toward native. A cross-platform framework often fits when the screens are mostly forms, status, and document capture. We make the call after the first workshop and write down why. 02 How does offline proof of delivery work? The app saves the photo or signature on the phone, shows it as queued, and retries when signal returns. The driver sees what has uploaded and what is still waiting, and dispatch sees the same state. We agree conflict rules with you, such as what happens if the load was reassigned while the phone was offline. 03 Can the app connect to our TMS or dispatch system? Usually, as far as the TMS exposes. We read the API docs and test with your account first. If the TMS has no usable API, an intermediate service or scheduled sync is the fallback, and we tell you the limits before the build starts. 04 Who publishes the app to the App Store and Google Play? You do, from your own Apple and Google developer accounts, with our help preparing builds and store listings. Review times are set by Apple and Google, so we plan the launch around them rather than promise a date. 05 Can you design only, or also build the app? Both are possible. Some teams want designs and prototypes for their own mobile engineers. Others want design and build together. Either way the backend contract is designed alongside the screens, because most mobile problems start there. START A CONVERSATION Show us the task drivers still do by phone call or paper.
