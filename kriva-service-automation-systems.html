Logistics and ops workflow automation

Automate the handoffs. Keep people on exceptions.

Workflow automation for carriers, brokers, and B2B ops teams: load-status updates, document intake, detention flags, invoice triggers, and support triage. Built in Make, Zapier, or custom code, with an approval step before anything customer-facing goes out.

Who this automation service is for, what we deliver, and where it stops

Who it's for

Dispatch, billing, and support teams at carriers, brokers, and B2B software companies. People who already repeat the same steps between email, a spreadsheet, a TMS or CRM, and an accounting tool.

What we deliver

A workflow map, the built scenarios (Make, Zapier, or custom workers), approval steps, a queue for failed runs, and documentation your team can run without us.

Not this service

Connector architecture between systems sits under Integrations & APIs. Month-end reconciliation sits under QuickBooks & Xero. Building the product with AI tools sits under AI-Assisted Development.

We map the loop as it runs today, automate the safe steps, and leave people on the ones that cost money when wrong.

Trucking and logistics scenarios

What we usually automate first.

These are patterns we scope with you against your own systems, not packaged products. Each one has a step where a person decides.

Load status and check calls

  • Status updates land on the load record
  • A missed check call opens a task for the dispatcher
  • The dispatcher, not the workflow, decides to reassign

POD and BOL intake

  • Documents from email or upload matched to the load
  • Low-confidence matches go to a review list
  • Missing-document flags before billing runs

Detention and accessorials

  • Arrival and release times compared to the appointment window
  • A draft claim is prepared for the billing team
  • Nothing goes to the broker until someone approves it

Invoice and settlement triggers

  • Delivered load plus verified POD prepares an invoice draft
  • Approval step before the write to the ledger
  • QuickBooks Online or Xero updated through their APIs

How we work

Write the loop down before automating it.

We document the loop as it actually runs, including the spreadsheet and the group chat, before a single scenario is built.

  1. 01 · Map

    Trigger, system, exception

    What starts the run, which system owns the record, where people already step in, and what a failure should look like.

  2. 02 · Build

    Gates and fallbacks

    Scenarios built with an approval step on anything customer-facing or ledger-facing, and a queue where failed runs land.

  3. 03 · Hand over

    Your team runs it

    Accounts, naming, rollback steps, and a runbook. Nothing stays a black box that only we can open.

Platforms

Make, Zapier, or custom code.

The loop decides the platform. We look at volume, connector coverage, data handling terms, and who will maintain it.

Make & Zapier

Often the right start when connectors exist and your team will own the scenarios. Quick to change, easy to inspect.

Make Zapier
  • Webhook and email triggers with visible error states
  • Routing and notifications for dispatch and billing
  • Approval step before customer-facing send

Custom workers

When run limits, data handling rules, or the approval screen outgrow what the platform can host.

  • Queue-backed workers with safe retries
  • LLM steps with a fallback to a person
  • An operator screen to approve, hold, and re-run

Approval and monitoring

Approve and Hold are part of the design from day one. A failed run returns to a queue someone is assigned to check.

  • Review queues for send, claim, and ledger steps
  • Named owner for each scenario
  • Rollback and pause steps in the runbook

Scope

What you get, what we need, where it stops.

Deliverables

Workflow map, built and tested scenarios, approval screens or channels, failure queue, runbook, and a handover session with the people who will run it.

What you provide

Admin access or a test account for each system, a few real (sanitized) examples of the messages and documents involved, and one person who can decide what needs approval.

Limits

We cannot automate through a system that blocks API or export access. We do not promise accuracy for AI steps; we design the fallback and test on your samples. Judgment calls stay with your team.

FAQ

Automation questions.

What teams ask before a first call, especially when the brief mixes Make, Zapier, and a TMS.

Not for anything that carries money, a commitment, or a complaint. Status copies, internal flags, and document filing can run without a person. A customer-facing message, a detention claim, or an invoice waits in an approval queue until someone on your team sends it. If you want one narrow message to go automatically, we scope it with explicit rules and an off switch.

Start with work that is repeated, rule-based, and cheap to get wrong: copying load status into a sheet, filing PODs against the right load, flagging a missed check call. Leave judgment calls, such as reassigning a late load or waiving detention, with your dispatchers and billing team. We confirm the list during the workflow map.

Make or Zapier when the volume, connectors, and approval steps fit inside the platform and your team will maintain it. Custom workers when run limits, data handling, or the approval console outgrow the platform. We check run limits, connector coverage, and data terms before recommending one.

It happens often. We read the vendor's API docs and your account permissions first. If there is no usable API, the fallbacks are scheduled exports, email parsing, or a small import step, each with more failure modes. If none of those is reliable enough, we will say the step should stay manual.

This service designs the queue: triggers, approvals, and what happens when a run fails. Integrations & APIs covers connector architecture between systems. AI-Assisted Development covers how AI is used to build and inside your product. We may use an LLM step inside a scenario, with a person approving the output.

Your team. The scenarios live in your Make, Zapier, or hosting accounts. We hand over the workflow map, naming, run-failure queue, and a short runbook. Ongoing changes can stay in-house, or we can agree a small support arrangement if you ask for one.
