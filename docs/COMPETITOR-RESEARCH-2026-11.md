Nothing on the KRIVA site copies these companies' words, layouts, images, or code. This file records why their approaches work, so KRIVA can answer the same buyer questions in its own way.

## What competitors do better than KRIVA did

1. **Say what they sell in the headline.** DreamzTech and MetaDesign state the service outright. KRIVA's "One load record, from tender to books" was clever, but a first-time visitor had to decode it.
2. **Explain build vs buy, features, integrations, and cost on one page.** Mind Studios and Matellio answer the buying questions in depth. KRIVA had the guides but did not connect them from its main pages.
3. **Give every buyer type a landing page.** Competitors have dedicated freight broker, TMS, and fleet pages. KRIVA had none for freight brokers or fleet management, which are named priorities.
4. **Show both CTAs together, plus a phone-friendly way in.** MetaDesign keeps both a call and a project path visible.
5. **Use third-party proof.** Clutch ratings and certifications do a lot of the work. **KRIVA has none of these yet and must not imply it does.** This is an off-site job for the owner, listed in the report.
6. **Publish a visible process and engagement options.** KRIVA has these, but they were buried on `/process`.

## What KRIVA was missing

- A headline that states the offer.
- A route for each buyer type (carrier, broker, SaaS founder, finance lead).
- Freight broker and fleet management pages.
- Timelines and engagement models near the top of the funnel.
- Security, QA, and support information. Competitors badge these; KRIVA barely mentioned them.
- A named, accountable person on the homepage.
- Complete, official QuickBooks and Xero logos (the Xero file was a hand-drawn cross).
- A working form. Production forms were failing (see the report). No amount of design matters until that is fixed.

## Where competitors are weak, and how KRIVA can differ

| Their weakness | What KRIVA does instead |
|---|---|
| Hero art is generic: glowing icons over stock trucks, or cartoons | Shows a working dispatch board visitors can actually click |
| Claims stacked on claims (500+, 98%, 4.9) | Publishes only what is verified, and says which case has signed-off numbers |
| Everything for everyone: AI, SAP, AR/VR, gaming, real estate | Narrow focus: trucking, SaaS, and accounting integrations |
| Sales-led first contact | The people building the product take the first call, and "don't build it" is a possible answer |
| Build-first bias | States honestly when a boxed TMS is the better buy |
| Same red or orange CTA, same heavy sans | A distinct look: road-signage type and interstate-sign green |

## What was deliberately not adopted

- **Stat rails and badge walls.** KRIVA does not have the numbers or certificates. Inventing them is off limits, and they would be easy to disprove.
- **"Hire developers in India" keywords.** This attracts staff-augmentation shoppers, not the owner-operators KRIVA wants.
- **City and state location pages.** With no local office or local proof, they would be thin doorway pages. See the market note in the report.
- **4,000-word guides on every page.** Service pages stay readable, and depth lives in the guides.
