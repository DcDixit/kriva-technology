Stage 01 · Load received

A tender arrives and has to be readable in three seconds.

Lane, weight, pickup window, accessorials. If a dispatcher has to open three tabs to decide whether to take it, the interface has already failed.

Stage 02 · Dispatcher assignment

Assignment is a bulk action, not a per-row chore.

Multi-select across the board, apply once, audit as a single event. This is the pattern that moved handle time on FleetFlow.

Stage 03 · Driver confirmation

Acceptance happens on a phone, in a yard, on bad signal.

Load acceptance, status updates, documents, and communication on the road, designed for gloves, glare, and one hand.

Stage 04 · In transit

Fleet visibility that answers the customer's question.

Real-time fleet visibility and route performance, designed for dispatchers and operations managers, not for a screenshot in a pitch deck.

Stage 05 · Exception detected

The exception surfaces before the customer calls.

Detention, breakdown, missed window. The board raises it against the SLA clock so the queue gets worked by urgency, not arrival order.

Stage 06 · Supervisor action

Overrides exist, and every one is audited.

Supervisor oversight built for ops floors: who changed what, when, and why, recoverable if it was the wrong call.

Stage 07 · Delivered

POD captured, invoice ready, ledger in sync.

Delivery closes the loop into accounting. We connect ELD, mapping, accounting, and CRM systems with reliable sync and monitoring.
