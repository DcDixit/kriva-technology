Operational problems

The TMS records the load. The workaround runs the operation.

KRIVA is a custom product-design and engineering partner, not an off-the-shelf TMS vendor. These are the gaps we see before a rebuild is even on the table.

Dispatch queue

Spreadsheet dispatch queues

The TMS holds the load record. Assignments, priorities, and the real queue still live in a shared sheet dispatch trusts more.

Status chasing

Manual status chasing

Dispatch calls drivers, checks chat, and refreshes reports because status in the system lags what the floor already knows.

Exception timing

Late exception visibility

SLA risk surfaces after the pickup window slips or the customer calls, not on the row where the dispatcher can act.

Disconnected driver documents

POD photos, signatures, and stop updates sit on a phone or in email until someone manually closes the loop in dispatch and accounting.

Duplicate billing or accounting work

Finance re-enters accessorials and POD proof because the TMS, spreadsheet, and QuickBooks or Xero never stayed in sync.

Integrations

The new product has to work with the systems already running the business.

We extend or integrate with your TMS, telematics, maps, and finance stack, not replace everything on day one.

Integrations & APIs→
  • Existing TMS or CRM
  • ELD and telematics
  • Maps, routing, and tracking
  • Driver documents and POD
  • QuickBooks, Xero & finance
  • APIs, webhooks, and internal services

Reliability operators can trust

  • Visible failed syncs, not silent drift into month-end
  • Retry and reconciliation states finance can read
  • Queued offline actions from driver devices
  • Audit history on overrides and bulk events
  • Permissioned supervisor actions
  • Clear human resolution paths, not raw API errors

Keep, extend, or build

Build only where the workflow creates an advantage.

Custom software is useful when the operation outgrew the system, not when a vendor demo looked easier.

Keep or configure

  • Current TMS handles the main workflow effectively
  • Integrations are stable enough to trust
  • Workarounds are minor, not operation-critical
  • User adoption is acceptable across roles

Extend or integrate

  • Current system stays the source of record
  • One role, workflow, or integration needs improvement
  • Dispatch desk or driver app is the gap, not the whole stack
  • Phased rollout can run beside the existing TMS

Build

  • Spreadsheets run core dispatch decisions
  • Exceptions stay hidden until too late
  • Existing system cannot support the business model
  • Settlements or integrations are the product

Delivery & migration

Change the system without stopping the dispatch desk.

Shadow the workflow, prototype with operators, integrate beside the current stack, and cut over when dispatch signs off.

Full process→
  1. 01 · Shadow

    Shadow and map

    Sit with dispatch. Map the board, exceptions, handoffs, and workarounds before anything is designed.

  2. 02 · Prototype

    Prototype the workflow

    Review realistic interfaces with operators, speed, error recovery, and high-volume actions first.

  3. 03 · Build

    Build and integrate

    Connect TMS, ELD, maps, and finance with retries and audit trails ops will accept.

  4. 04 · Pilot

    Pilot or run in parallel

    Feature flags and parallel workflows so the current operation keeps running.

  5. 05 · Cutover

    Cut over and improve

    Phased launch, operator docs, and iteration after the desk is live on the new path.
