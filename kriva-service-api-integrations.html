Connectors that hold after the first sync.

Bitbucket, ledger, CRM, and payment APIs through one structure: a stable event, a documented write, and an exception someone can finish.

Why the demo is not the product

Staging success is not a production connector

A Bitbucket webhook or a QuickBooks invoice that “worked once” still fails on retries, mapping drift, and errors nobody owns.

01

No owner when a record fails

The job reports success or a raw 500. Production needs a named person, a source record, and a next step.

02

Retries without a stable key

A timeout posts twice. Without idempotency, every retry makes the destination worse than the miss.

03

Errors operators cannot act on

JSON blobs and HTTP codes. Ops needs source value, destination value, and Retry, Match, or Park.

Integration structure

One path. Every API.

Bitbucket Pipelines, ledger posts, and CRM writes share the same sequence. The connector is finished when failure is visible, owned, and auditable.

  1. 01
    Source eventQueued with a stable key from the product, Bitbucket, or another system of record.
  2. 02
    ConnectorWorkers, backoff, and documented endpoints. No CSV dumps as the default.
  3. 03
    ValidateSchema, mapping, and idempotency before the destination write.
  4. 04
    Destination APIBitbucket, QuickBooks, Xero, Stripe, HubSpot, or a scoped industry API.
  5. 05
    Exception or auditMatch confirms agreement, or an operator finishes the record without an engineering ticket.

When the write fails

Operators finish what APIs cannot

Illustration Operator pattern · no client data

A Bitbucket pipeline miss and a ledger mismatch use the same language: source, destination, reason, owner, action.

  • Source recordStable event ID and payload reference, not a log line buried in a worker.
  • Readable reasonMapping mismatch, rate limit, or validation fail — in language ops can act on.
  • Named resolutionRetry, Match, or Park. Owner, timestamp, and an audit state when it closes.

How the work is owned

Map, then build, then operate

Three phases. Each has a definition of done. Code does not start until systems, records, and retry policy are signed off.

  1. 01 · Map

    Systems, records, owners

    What posts where, who is paged, what “done” means, and how idempotency works.

  2. 02 · Build

    Documented APIs

    Bitbucket, ledger, payments, CRM, and platform APIs through native connectors.

  3. 03 · Operate

    Exception tooling

    Retry, match, and resolution surfaces so the connector does not become a ticket pile.
