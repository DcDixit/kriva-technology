Dispatch CRM solutions for US trucking companies: what actually works

Four patterns that work when dispatch has outgrown the TMS but cannot live without it: bulk assignment, SLA on the row, role-based density, and a phased rollout the floor can absorb.

May 2026 8 min read KRIVA Logistics Product Team
Trucking & Logistics

The TMS holds the record. The spreadsheet runs the shift. A dispatch CRM has to close that gap.

8 min read · May 2026

US carriers post loads in the TMS because billing, compliance, and customer records live there. Dispatchers run the shift in a spreadsheet because that is where bulk reassignment, detention notes, and SLA burndown actually happen. The gap is not missing software. It is two systems of record, reconciled after the shift instead of during it.

1. Bulk assignment is one event, not forty clicks

When volume crosses a few hundred daily routes, per-row assignment becomes the bottleneck. Select the loads, pick the driver or lane owner, confirm once. The supervisor gets one audit trail, not forty silent edits spread across a sheet tab. If your TMS cannot batch reassignment with a reason code, the desk will keep doing it elsewhere.

2. Put SLA on the row, not in a separate dashboard

Exception dashboards that live three clicks away get checked once an hour. SLA remaining belongs on every load row, color-weighted, sorted by urgency. Dispatchers should answer "what breaks in the next fifteen minutes?" from the board they already have open, not from a chart they have to hunt for. When we rebuilt ShiftRail's console for a 400-route US desk, moving burndown onto the row cut average exception response to eleven minutes.

3. Match density to the role, not the demo screen

A dispatcher scanning 120 rows needs compact type, sticky headers, and keyboard-friendly multi-select. A field supervisor on a tablet needs thumb-sized actions and fewer rows per screen. An executive summary can be sparse. One layout for every persona means someone is squinting or scrolling. Shadow three shifts before you pick column density: the person doing bulk work at 6am is not the person signing off overrides at 2pm.

4. Roll out in phases the floor can absorb

Do not swap TMS, spreadsheet, and CRM on the same Monday. Week one: dispatch board with read-only TMS sync. Week two: bulk assignment and SLA on the row. Week three: exception queue and supervisor overrides with audit. Integrations and analytics after daily usage proves out. Measure handle time per load and exception response from day one, not after a quarter of parallel running.
