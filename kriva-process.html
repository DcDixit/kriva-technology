Who is in the room

We start with the people who will use it.

Discovery is a working session with the desk, not only the sponsor who commissioned the project. A dispatcher, an operator, or a finance lead walks the current workflow. That conversation becomes the written brief you sign before design starts.

  • The people who will use the product are in the session
  • The brief names scope, constraints, and success metrics
  • You can keep the brief even if you do not continue

Five phases. Hard gates.

    Phase 01 · 1–2 weeks You sign off

    You leave with A scoped brief and a realistic timeline

  2. Design

    W3–7

    Phase 02 · 2–5 weeks You sign off

    Flows, prototypes, and high-fidelity UI are reviewed in weekly demos. Product and UI/UX design and prototyping finish here. Engineering does not start until the pack is signed. A change after this gate is a change request, not a surprise.

    You leave with Clickable UI the people who will use it can try

  3. Build

    Weekly

    Phase 03 · weekly demos

    Incremental development with QA gates and open progress. You see working software every week on a real URL, not a big reveal. Regression checks run before each demo. Tools can speed the work. A person still reviews what ships.

    You leave with Shippable increments you can click

  4. Launch

    Go-live

    Phase 04 Go-live checklist

    Deployment, analytics, handoff docs, and training for the people who will run it. We do not call it live until the checklist is complete and someone on your side can operate it.

    You leave with A live product and a named owner

  5. Support

    Optional

    Phase 05 · optional retainer

    Iteration sprints, Workflow automation maintenance, and roadmap support. You can stay on a monthly retainer after launch, or take the handoff. It is a choice, not a lock-in buried in the statement of work.

    You leave with A clear retain-or-handoff decision
