KRIVA product design and development process

Who it's for

Founders and ops leads building trucking software, B2B SaaS, or finance integrations — who will join a weekly demo and sign a written brief before design or code starts.

What you receive

A scoped brief, signed UI pack, weekly working demos on a real URL, a go-live checklist, and an optional retainer path — each with a named deliverable and owner.

How we ensure quality

Hard sign-off gates before design and build. QA checkpoints in every build sprint. One decision-maker on each side. No phase starts on a handshake.

Same sequence for trucking consoles, SaaS products, and finance connectors. Capabilities under Services; stack under Tools & stack. AI-assisted development speeds work inside these phases — people still sign what ships.

How a project runs

Five phases. Hard gates.

We do not start the next phase on a handshake. Discover and Design have hard sign-off. Build has weekly demos and QA gates. Launch has a go-live checklist. Support is optional and named.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Goals, users, and constraints in a written brief you sign off.

  2. 02

    Design

    Flows, prototypes, and UI pack reviewed in weekly demos.

  3. 03

    Build

    Incremental delivery with QA gates on a live URL.

  4. 04

    Launch

    Deployment, handoff docs, and go-live checklist.

  5. 05

    Support

    Optional retainer for iteration and maintenance.

  1. W1–2

    Discover

    Phase 01 · 1–2 weeks You sign off

    Strategy, scope, users, constraints, and success metrics in a written brief. We sit with the desk that will use the product — often starting with UX research or workflow mapping, not only the sponsor who commissioned it.

    You leave with A scoped brief and a realistic timeline

  2. W3–7

    Design

    Phase 02 · 2–5 weeks You sign off

    Flows, prototypes, and high-fidelity UI reviewed in weekly demos. Product design and prototyping finish here — engineering does not start until the pack is signed. Changes after this gate are a change request, not a surprise.

    You leave with Clickable UI the floor can react to

  3. Weekly

    Build

    Phase 03 · weekly demos

    Incremental development with QA gates and open progress. You see working software every week on a real URL, not a big reveal. Regression checks run before each demo. Tools accelerate the work — a person still reviews what ships.

    You leave with Shippable increments, not a black box

  4. Go-live

    Launch

    Phase 04 Go-live checklist

    Deployment, analytics, handoff docs, and training for the people who will run it. We do not call it live until the checklist is green and someone on your side can operate it.

    You leave with A live product and a named owner

  5. Optional

    Support

    Phase 05 · optional retainer

    Iteration sprints, automation maintenance, and roadmap support. Most clients stay on a monthly retainer after launch. It is a choice, not a lock-in buried in the SOW.

    You leave with A clear retain-or-handoff decision

Cadence

How the week actually feels

Ahmedabad, India. Remote-first. Overlap windows for UK and US clients so the demo is a conversation, not a recording.

01

Weekly demo, written next step

Every week you see the work and leave with the next decision named. No status theater. If a gate is blocked, we say so on the call.

02

UK and US hours, on purpose

Demos sit in the overlap. Async updates land in writing the same day. You are not expected to stay up for India midnight.

03

One owner on each side

You name a decision-maker. We name a lead. Feedback that is not from the owner waits. That is how sign-off stays real.
