Who SEO is for, what people search for, and what we deliver

Who it’s for

Carriers, brokers, and 3PLs with a marketing site, and freight-tech and SaaS vendors with product pages, whose site is hard to find or hard to measure. Typically teams without an in-house search specialist.

What people search

Buyers comparing dispatch or TMS software, operators checking whether a tool integrates with QuickBooks or Xero, and shippers vetting a carrier. These are examples of intent, not a keyword list we promise to win.

What we deliver

A crawl and index audit, page structure mapped to buyer questions, page briefs, schema, and GA4 and Search Console set up so form fills and calls show up as events.

KRIVA is a product design and engineering studio. SEO is a supporting service, strongest when it sits next to a site or product we build. You keep account access and documentation.

Where it breaks

Three common gaps on logistics and SaaS sites.

We name the failure before we publish another page.

01

Product pages describe features, not the job

A TMS or dispatch page that never says what a dispatcher or broker is trying to do gives search engines and buyers little to match.COST: the right buyer cannot tell the page is for them

02

Thin, duplicated lane and service pages

Near-identical pages for each lane, city, or service, with duplicate URLs and no canonical.COST: pages compete with each other

03

Quote requests are not tracked as events

Form fills, calls, and demo requests are not recorded in GA4, or they fire twice.COST: nobody knows which pages produce inquiries

What we do

Three jobs, in this order.

Structure first, then content, then measurement and paid search.

01 · Site

Technical and structure

  • Crawl, index, and canonical audit
  • Sitemap, internal links, and schema
  • Page templates and speed issues, flagged for your developer
02 · Content

Intent and page briefs

  • Pages mapped to buyer questions
  • Briefs, outlines, and on-page copy for key pages
  • Expert review loop so claims stay accurate
03 · Measure

Tracking and paid search

  • GA4 and Search Console set up correctly
  • Conversion events checked against the CRM or form
  • Google Ads tracking audit before spend scales

Method

Baseline, fix, report.

Fix the pipes before adding pages or spend.

  1. 01

    Baseline

    We review what is indexed, what is tracked, and which pages get inquiries. You provide: Search Console and GA4 access, CMS access, and a list of your top services or products.

  2. 02

    Fix and structure

    Technical fixes, page structure, and briefs for the pages that matter. Your developer or ours makes the changes.

  3. 03

    Publish and report

    Pages go live after expert review, then a regular readout shows what moved, what did not, and what ships next.

What you receive

Evidence you can open yourself.

Search Console, GA4, and ad account access, not a purchased rank screenshot. A written list of what moved, what did not, and what ships next.

Audit
Index coverage, canonicals, and the pages that carry the site.
Briefs
Outlines and on-page copy for key pages, tied to a buyer question.
Tracking
Events that match the form or CRM, so inquiries can be traced to a page.
Readout
Plain-language summary with the next actions named. No invented numbers.

We do not guarantee rankings, traffic, or lead volume. Search results depend on factors we do not control, and we have no published SEO case study to cite.
