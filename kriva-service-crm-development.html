The desk

The work that never makes the system of record.

Dispatch, sales, and account teams keep a second system because the official one cannot show the owner, the time left, and the next action in one place. A custom CRM is that missing screen, built on your records and your rules.

Scope

Who it is for, what you get, and where we stop.

01

Who it's for

Dispatch managers, ops leads, and brokerage teams at US carriers and logistics companies whose TMS holds the load but a spreadsheet runs the shift. Also B2B sales and service teams whose CRM records the deal but not the work around it.

02

What we deliver

  • Role-based views and permissions
  • Pipelines and stage rules
  • Bulk actions and exception queues
  • Workflow automation with approval steps
  • Customer, account, and load records with history
  • Reporting and an audit trail
03

Where it stops

We are a design and engineering studio, not a CRM or TMS vendor. Your TMS or CRM stays the system of record unless you choose otherwise.

System of record stays yours

Where teams work around the system

The real work should not live beside the system.

When bulk actions, clear ownership, and role-based views are missing, people build workarounds. Each one has a cost.

01

A spreadsheet beside the TMS or CRM

Assignments, follow-ups, appointment times, and SLA tracking live in a shared sheet because the system of record cannot show them together.

Costdouble entry, no audit trail, stale records

02

One record, one click, forty times

Reassigning a lane after a breakdown, or moving a departing rep’s accounts, means editing records one at a time.

Costbacklog, supervisor rework

03

Nobody owns the exception

A late load, a missing proof of delivery, or a stalled deal sits in a report nobody opens until the customer calls.

Costproblems surface late, customers call first

What we build

Different desks. One shared record.

Everyone works from the same record. What changes by role is density, fields, and the actions within reach.

Dispatch desk

Multi-select, one approval, one audit entry. Exceptions sorted by time remaining, with owner and next action on the row.

Field supervisors

The desk needs density. A supervisor in the yard needs a few large actions. Same record, different layout, audited overrides.

Billing and settlement

See which delivered loads are held up by a missing document or an unapproved accessorial, and what to send to QuickBooks or Xero once they clear.

Sales and account teams

Stage definitions with exit criteria, rep and manager views, and forecast screens, as in our CRMPulse HubSpot example. Useful for brokers, carriers, and B2B software teams alike.

How we work

Use the current system until the workflow proves it needs more.

Not every desk needs a custom build. We look at the work first and recommend the smallest change that holds.

Three ways to engageSmallest changeLargest change

Configure

Tune the CRM you have

Fields, views, permissions, and reports in HubSpot or a similar CRM, when its native bulk actions already cover the job.

Extend

Add a console beside it

Keep the TMS or CRM as the record. Add a dispatch board, exception queue, or automation where one workflow is the gap.

Rebuild

Replace a module that blocks the work

When spreadsheets carry core work and the platform cannot support bulk actions or audited overrides, we replace one module at a time.

Delivery process

Map the workflow before changing the system.

Six steps, from watching the desk to a phased rollout. You see working software on a live URL while it is built, and nothing changes for the whole team at once.

  1. 01 · Map

    Watch the desk

    Shadow dispatchers and supervisors. Write down handoffs, workarounds, and what the system is supposed to control.

  2. 02 · Friction

    Find the friction

    Locate where side sheets, missing permissions, or missing bulk actions push work outside the system.

  3. 03 · Design

    Prototype with the desk

    Role-based views, bulk actions, and exception handling tested with the people who will use them.

  4. 04 · Build

    Build and connect

    Console, connectors, and automation beside the existing stack, shown regularly on a live URL.

  5. 05 · Validate

    Test with operators

    Bulk actions and edge cases run against sanitized data shaped like yours, not only demo records.

  6. 06 · Roll out

    Phase it in

    Cutover by role or module. Code, designs, and documentation move to your accounts.

FAQ

CRM development questions.

Short answers on scope, timelines, cost, and what we need before a first call.

We assess first. If stages, permissions, and the CRM's own bulk actions already fit, configuration is the answer and nothing needs building. Replacement comes up only when the platform cannot support bulk work, role-based views, or audited overrides for the way your desk runs.

Not by default. The TMS stays the system of record and the new console runs beside it, rolled out by role or module. If you want to retire a TMS module later, we scope that as a separate step.

Usually, depending on what the TMS exposes. We check API access, export options, and your account permissions first. If there is no usable API, scheduled exports or an import step are the fallback, with more failure modes. We tell you before the build which case you are in.

Yes, when it is in scope. We map fields, run test imports on sanitized samples, and agree a cutover by role or module so the desk keeps working. Messy history sometimes needs a decision from your team about what to keep.

A day of watching the desk work, a sanitized export of recent loads or deals, sandbox or admin access to the CRM and TMS, and one person per role who can say whether a screen works for them.

Designs, code, and documentation transfer to your accounts. Changes after go-live can stay with your team, or we can agree a support retainer if you want one. We do not host your data unless we scope hosting as part of the project.

Discovery takes 1 to 2 weeks and ends with a scoped brief and timeline you sign off. A focused first version, such as one console or one pipeline, is usually 6 to 10 weeks after that. A larger build with several integrations usually runs 12 to 20 weeks.

Cost follows scope: team size multiplied by weeks. We price after discovery, as a fixed-scope project, sprints, or a monthly retainer. Our guide to custom dispatch software cost explains what drives the budget.
