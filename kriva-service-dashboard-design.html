Who this dashboard design service is for, what we deliver, and where it stops

Who it's for

Dispatch and fleet managers, billing teams, and ops leaders whose morning scan happens in a spreadsheet. Also SaaS product teams whose admin panel shows everything and prioritizes nothing.

What we deliver

Role-based dashboard designs with a clear hierarchy, filters that land on the right records, exception queues with inline actions, and specs your engineers can build from. We can build the views as well.

Where it stops

We design what the data can support. If a source has no API or the numbers disagree, that becomes a finding, not a chart. We are not a BI vendor and do not resell dashboard software.

Designs, code, and documentation transfer to your accounts after launch.

Why dashboards go unused

The data exists.
The next move is still unclear.

01

Equal-weight charts

On-time rate, miles, and open loads share one row. Nothing says which load needs a phone call in the next ten minutes.COST: slow decisions, exceptions found by the customer

02

Widget sprawl

Every manager asked for one more chart. Dispatch, billing, and sales share a screen that serves none of them.COST: cognitive overload, people stop opening it

03

The scan lives in a spreadsheet

Someone exports the load list each morning, colors the risky rows, and shares the sheet. The dashboard is only where the numbers come from.COST: double entry, stale data, no audit trail

Decision flow

Question, signal, records, action.

Each dashboard answers one question for one role. For dispatch it might be which loads will miss their appointment. For billing, which delivered loads cannot be invoiced yet and why. The signal shows what needs attention, the records give evidence, and the action sits on the row.

Role-based views

Dispatch, fleet, billing, and accounts need different screens.

Same underlying data, different first question. We design each view for the decision that role makes.

Dispatch: late and at-risk loads first

  • Time remaining and owner on every row
  • Detention and missed-appointment flags in the queue
  • Reassign or resolve without leaving the view
  • Filters by lane, terminal, or customer

Fleet and ops managers: one signal, then drill-down

  • A single primary indicator at the top
  • A short trend, not twelve competing charts
  • Exception count that links to the records
  • Drill-down that keeps the filters you set

Billing and finance: what blocks an invoice

  • Missing PODs and unapproved accessorials first
  • Sync status to QuickBooks or Xero by period
  • Row-level detail for audit questions
  • Links back to the source record

Accounts and sales: stalled work and next actions

  • Stage density and stalled deals above the fold
  • Owner on every row
  • Next-action prompts, not passive lists
  • Forecast trend as supporting context
