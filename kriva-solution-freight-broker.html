At a glance

What is custom freight broker software?

Freight broker software is the system a brokerage uses to quote shipper freight, find and book carriers, track loads in transit, collect documents, bill customers, and pay carriers. A custom build shapes those screens around how your desk actually works, instead of the workflow a packaged TMS assumes.

Who it's for
US freight brokerages and 3PLs whose quoting, carrier sales, or customer service has outgrown spreadsheets and inbox workarounds.
What we build
Quoting and customer tools, carrier sales and onboarding, a load tracking board, documents and billing, and margin reporting.
How it fits
Usually beside the TMS you already run, connected through its API or data export. Full replacement only when it clearly pays off.
Who builds it
KRIVA, a product design and engineering studio. You own the code, designs, and documentation.

The problem

Where broker desks lose time and margin

  1. 01

    Quotes live in email

    Rates come from memory, a spreadsheet, and a load board. Nobody can see which quotes won, on which lanes, or why.

  2. 02

    Carrier sales is a phone list

    Your best carriers sit in one broker's notes. When that broker is out, the relationship goes with them.

  3. 03

    Check calls eat the afternoon

    Status lives in texts and calls, so customers ask where their freight is before your team knows.

  4. 04

    Carrier checks happen late

    Authority and insurance get reviewed in a rush, after the load is already promised to the shipper.

  5. 05

    Paperwork holds up cash

    Rate confirmations, proof of delivery, and invoices sit in different places, so billing waits on someone finding a PDF.

  6. 06

    Margin shows up at month-end

    You find out which customers and lanes made money long after you could have acted on it.

How the software follows a load

One load record, from quote to carrier pay

Each step below writes to the same load, so the next person never has to ask what happened. Your build may cover all of them or start with the two that hurt most.

  1. 01

    Quote

    Price the lane from your own quote history and the rate sources you already use. Won and lost quotes are logged by customer and lane.

  2. 02

    Book

    An accepted quote or a customer tender becomes a load, including tenders that arrive by EDI where the shipper sends them.

  3. 03

    Cover

    Offer the load to carriers who have run the lane before. Every call, offer, and counter-rate is recorded on the load.

  4. 04

    Vet

    Authority, insurance, and your own carrier rules are checked before the rate confirmation goes out, not after.

  5. 05

    Track

    Check calls and tracking updates land on the load. Late pickups and missed appointments rise to the top of the board.

  6. 06

    Deliver

    Proof of delivery is attached to the load, along with accessorials such as detention or lumper fees.

  7. 07

    Bill and pay

    The customer invoice and the carrier payable come from the same record, then sync to QuickBooks or Xero.

The carrier and dock side

Updates should come from the freight, not from a check call

Pickups slip, appointments move, and a bill of lading gets signed with the wrong count. The broker usually hears last. We build the carrier-facing pieces that close that gap, so updates and paperwork arrive from the people handling the freight.

  • Load acceptance and rate confirmation sign-off by link
  • Status and ETA updates from the carrier, written to the load
  • BOL and proof of delivery uploaded from the dock or cab

An honest answer

Should a brokerage build or buy?

Buy a TMS if your freight and process are fairly standard. A good off-the-shelf product is cheaper and quicker. Build when your edge is how you quote, cover, or serve a niche, and the software keeps forcing workarounds. Many brokerages land in the middle: keep the TMS as the record, and build the desk tools around it.

How is custom broker software different from a TMS?

A TMS is a packaged system that covers the standard broker workflow for many companies at once. Custom broker software is built around one brokerage's quoting, carrier sales, and customer service. Many brokerages use both: the TMS stays the record, and custom tools handle the parts the TMS makes awkward.

Can this work with our existing TMS?

Usually, yes, if the TMS offers an API or data export. We build beside it and only replace parts of it when that clearly pays off. We check what your TMS allows during discovery.

Can you connect to load boards, tracking providers, and EDI?

Where the provider offers an API or EDI connection and your plan includes access. Access rules vary by provider, so we confirm this before scoping rather than assuming it.

Can you automate carrier onboarding and compliance checks?

We can build the onboarding packet, document capture, insurance expiry reminders, and checks against public FMCSA data or a monitoring service you subscribe to. Your compliance owner sets the rules, and we do not give legal advice.

How long does a first version take?

A focused first version is usually 6 to 10 weeks after a 1 to 2 week discovery. A full platform with integrations usually runs 12 to 20 weeks.

How much does custom freight broker software cost?

Cost follows scope: team size multiplied by weeks. We price after discovery, as a fixed-scope project, sprints, or a monthly retainer. Our guide to custom dispatch software cost explains what drives the budget.

Who owns the software?

You do. Code, designs, and documentation transfer to you at project close or earlier.
